Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in San Tan Valley

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our San Tan Valley crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.
