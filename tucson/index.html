Restoration Challenges Tucson Homeowners Face

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Tucson follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Tucson technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our San Tan Valley Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Tucson.

Preventing Water Damage in Tucson Homes

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Tucson homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Tucson living room.

Working With Your Insurer After Water Damage in Tucson

A claim is decided on what can be shown, which makes documentation the most useful tool a Tucson owner has. Begin collecting it before the cleanup and before anything goes in the trash.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Take wide shots of each room and close shots of damaged items, including appliance labels and serial numbers. Do not discard damaged carpet, furniture or contents until the adjuster has seen them or agreed to photos. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.

Insurers often draw a line between sudden damage, like a burst pipe, and damage from a slow leak or outside flooding. Where your loss falls is for the insurer or your agent to say, and it is not something we can promise. Our crew supplies the technical record, meaning moisture maps, drying logs and a plain description of what we found. Let us know early that your Tucson job involves a claim, and we will organize the paperwork around it.

What to Do Before Our Tucson Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Water from a broken supply line or an overflowing sink is the safest kind. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. Sewage and floodwater from outdoors are a health hazard, and the only job for you is to close the door and keep everyone out.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Tucson crew start faster. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Tucson leak seems small.
