Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in San Tan Valley

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

On every San Tan Valley job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.

What the Water Damages

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.
