Before anything else in a restoration project can work, the water has to be extracted. Structural materials, floors, possessions and building systems all take less damage when standing water comes out sooner. With each hour it sits, water works farther in: through carpet to the pad, up the drywall, into wood framing and down to the subfloor. For emergency water extraction in San Tan Valley, San Tan Valley Restoration brings truck-mounted units and portable extractors built to pull out hundreds of gallons every minute.

Truck-mounted units rank with the strongest water removal systems made, and their suction lifts water out of hard floors, carpet and structural materials much better than anything you can rent or buy. Where the truck hoses will not reach, we bring in submersible pumps, strong portable extractors and specialty tools made for water caught between floor layers, inside wall cavities or in tight spaces.

Professional water extraction is not simply about removing the standing water you can see. Our IICRC-certified technicians use moisture detection equipment to identify water that has migrated into hidden areas: behind baseboards, inside wall cavities, under cabinets and beneath flooring materials. Before drying starts, we use specialty tools to pull as much moisture as we can out of those hidden spots, so structural drying takes far less time and equipment and secondary mold is less likely.

What a Professional Brings to Water Extraction

Both the harm done to your home and the restoration bill depend directly on quick, thorough extraction. Professional truck-mounted extraction equipment removes water hundreds of times faster than consumer tools, so what might take you an entire day with a shop vacuum takes our equipment minutes. That gap in speed is more than a convenience, because each hour water touches building materials can add thousands of dollars in further damage.

Hidden moisture, the kind consumer tools never reach, is part of a professional extraction too. It follows plumbing lines and electrical conduits, slips between flooring layers, climbs drywall by wicking and is drawn into carpet pad through capillary action. We go after those hidden deposits with specialty tools and methods, and that takes out far more water than extracting the surface only. The payoff is a shorter drying period, lower equipment cost and, most important of all, less risk of mold growing unseen in trapped moisture.

Warning Signs for Water Extraction

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Any room where water has collected on the floor
  • Stairwells or window wells that fill up and spill water inside
  • Drips or a steady stream coming out of a ceiling, a wall or a fixture
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Wet baseboards and water creeping from beneath kitchen or bath cabinets
  • Laminate or wood flooring that feels spongy and pushes up water at the seams
  • Water accumulating after a fire suppression system discharges

How to Prevent Water Extraction Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Check the supply hoses on your appliances and swap out any that look old or worn
  • Test your sump pump before the rainy season so you know it will run
  • Make sure your home has working water leak sensors in critical areas
  • Shelving in low areas keeps belongings out of the water if the floor floods
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and label it clearly
  • Put together a simple kit with a bucket, some old towels and the phone number of your restoration company
