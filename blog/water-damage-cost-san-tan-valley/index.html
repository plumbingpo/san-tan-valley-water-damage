How Much Does Water Damage Cost in San Tan Valley?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in San Tan Valley?

The Quick Answer

Most water damage jobs in San Tan Valley fall somewhere between $1,500 and $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. Rebuilding after a sewage backup or a serious flood can push the cost far past $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Pricing in San Tan Valley, AZ depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. An exact price requires someone to inspect the home in person and take moisture readings.

Cost by Water Category

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. There are three categories in the industry standard, and each reflects a level of contamination. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. Here are the usual numbers.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterFailed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Sewage, river flooding, storm surgeAbout $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

The Main Price Factors

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Afterward comes a second cost for new flooring, drywall, paint and cabinets, which on larger jobs often exceeds the first.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in San Tan Valley. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. A washing machine, dishwasher or water heater leak caught early is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in San Tan Valley?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

A leak that developed slowly is usually excluded. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Do not guess: look at your policy documents or ask the agent who sold them. Your deductible also matters. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Keeping Your Costs Under Control

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. Some basic actions go a long way.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. Learn the location of the main valve ahead of time, and turn it off the moment a leak shows up.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Keep receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. Once it does, the job gets longer and more expensive.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. Saving money is never worth an infection or an electric shock. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

Turning a Range Into an Estimate

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.
