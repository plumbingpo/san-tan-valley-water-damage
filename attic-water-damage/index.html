Attic Water Damage in San Tan Valley

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. For San Tan Valley homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
How We Fix It

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Water Near Electrical

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Ceiling Collapse

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
